Mission Statement

In Opinion: On the Right to Confidential Healthcare

Healthcare is a human right. In order for that right to be exercised, access to healthcare must be protected.

This website is a resource for students dealing with a barrier to healthcare that often goes unnamed. And even if it is named, its story lies beyond the officiality of healthcare institutions. To overcome this barrier requires hours of research, stress, scrubbing across many different websites, and trying to piece together incongruent systems. This barrier is privacy and confidentiality: for adult dependents that rely on a parent’s insurance plan, the need to own one’s health doesn’t always sit comfortably with the reality of being a dependent, beholden to a parent’s potential control of your healthcare. But when healthcare systems assume the opposite reality– that health is a joint mission supervised and supported by a parent, rather than controlled– what are those facing the barrier meant to do?

Privacy and confidentiality can often feel taboo. Prioritizing your safety sometimes means keeping secrets from your parents. Maybe you don’t want them to know you got an STD test, because why would you need that if not for forbidden “promiscuity”? Or maybe you need counseling for something you are not yet ready to share, or medication for something you wish to remain private. Then does asking for help or support feel taboo as well? Maybe asking someone to help you keep a secret is embarrassing, or even worse, shameful. Shame can easily grow from a lack of information. Young adults often need confidentiality for the sake of their health, and yet, it is an issue that isn’t candidly discussed or officially parameterized in online healthcare resources. Who are our points of contact, anyway, if the websites don’t already explain how to navigate this– the receptionist or SHS doctor that has to see a hundred other patients today? It’s not exactly a problem or question with a quick solution. And it’s not the fault of these healthcare professionals either when the system, which combines insurance companies, digital portals, doctors, pharmacies, and more, isn’t built for stream-lined confidentiality. It’s something to navigate on your own. This website aims to change that, and to encourage open dialogue about how confidentiality is confusing, but more importantly, to address that it is a real public health problem; and one that can be ameliorated, even if only slightly.

Safe Care at UCSD is an independent, student-led health advocacy project.

This site provides educational information regarding California insurance privacy law and does not constitute legal or medical advice.
