Safe Care Clarifying privacy and confidentiality for health insurance at UCSD

Many college students rely on a parent’s health insurance. Whenever a healthcare service is used, these insurance companies send an Explanation Of Benefits (EOB) to policyholders. Who exactly that policyholder is, and where their EOB will be sent, can become confusing for adult dependents who live between home and university. Crucially, EOBs pose the risk of disclosing a dependent’s sensitive health information to their parents. When dependents seek sensitive care, such as, sexual, reproductive, or gender-affirming care, privacy concerns can prevent them from receiving the care that they need.

But your ability to make informed decisions for your health should not be prevented by privacy concerns.

Use this page to learn about your privacy options, secure your safety, and find support. California law protects your right to confidential healthcare.

Why privacy matters

1. Select Your Health Insurance Plan

Key Fact for UC SHIP

With UC SHIP, you are the student receiving coverage and the primary policyholder for your student plan. Your parents may help pay for the plan, but paying for insurance does not make them the person whose EOBs should be sent to them.

Step 1

Create your Anthem account

Find your UC SHIP member ID in MyChart under Insurance. Use that member ID to create or activate your Anthem account at anthem.com/ca (or in the Sydney Health app).

Step 2

Set EOBs to email only

In your Anthem account, open Profile → Communication Preferences, then choose email or digital-only delivery for EOBs. This avoids paper mail when the setting is available.

  • Navigate to Profile → Communication Preferences.
  • Check that the email address belongs to you and that the change was saved.
Step 3

If you want paper mail sent somewhere else

Anthem pulls the mailing address for this account from TritonLink. If you want paper EOBs sent to your San Diego address instead of your parents’ home, change your primary address in TritonLink → Personal Tools → Current Address. This is the address source Anthem uses for UC SHIP.

California Law Protection (SB 138 / CHIA)

If you are covered as a dependent on a parent’s plan, your parent may be the primary policyholder (the person who owns the plan), while you are the patient receiving care. Under California law, health plans must honor a Confidential Communications Request (CCR) for sensitive care and direct those communications to you instead of the primary policyholder.[3]

California’s confidentiality protections are set out in Civil Code §56.107 (AB 1184). If your plan is based outside California, HIPAA also gives you the right to request a different address or method for health-plan communications when disclosure could endanger you. Read the HHS guidance.

Step 1

Locate Your Insurance Card

Find the customer-service phone number on the back of your insurance card. Have your insurance account or member number ready.

Step 2

Submit a Confidential Communication Request

You can request a CCR by phone, mail, or email. Tell the insurer that you need confidentiality because you are receiving sensitive care and that sharing private information could lead to harm. You do not have to describe the specific care. UC SHIP students should use their Anthem account and TritonLink steps above instead of this script.

3. Ask before your appointment

Ask whether the visit is confidential, what it will cost, and whether bills, texts, or mail will be sent to anyone else.

Eligibility and prices vary, so confirm details directly with the provider.

2. Confidential Communication Request

Suggested wording

For people on a parent’s plan who need to request a CCR. You can make the request by phone, mail, or email. UC SHIP students can use the Anthem steps above instead.

Find the insurer
Use the customer-service phone number on the back of your insurance card, or sign in to the insurer’s website or member portal.
Ask for a CCR
Say you need confidential communications because you are receiving sensitive care and sharing private information could lead to harm. You do not need to give details about the care.
Choose a safe destination
Give an email address, mailing address, or phone number that only you can access. Ask when the request will take effect.

When you call your insurer

“Hi, my insurance account number is [YOUR ACCOUNT NUMBER]. I am going to receive sensitive healthcare and need a Confidential Communication Request form. Please send my EOBs and related communications to me at [YOUR EMAIL OR MAILING ADDRESS], rather than to the primary policyholder. I am the person receiving the care.”
